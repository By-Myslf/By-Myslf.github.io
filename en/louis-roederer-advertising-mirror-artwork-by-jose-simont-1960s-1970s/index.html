About this piece

The History of the Original Artwork

José Simont was a prominent illustrator of the Belle Époque period. He settled in Paris in 1898 and began working for the French magazine L’Illustration in 1902, remaining one of its leading illustrators for more than four decades.

During his career, Simont created several advertising illustrations for the Champagne Louis Roederer house.

The original composition featured on this mirror depicts an elegant scene from French high society at the beginning of the 20th century.

At the centre, a woman wearing an elaborate evening gown and jewellery is seated at a table. To her left, a formally dressed gentleman pours champagne into her glass, while another gentleman on her right raises a napkin to his face.

The detailed linework, restrained colours and refined portrayal of the figures reflect the characteristic style of José Simont’s early advertising illustrations.

Examples of the original composition from a numbered lithographic edition have also appeared at international art auctions.

The Advertising Mirror

The historical illustration was reproduced as a decorative advertising mirror, believed to have been manufactured during the 1960s–1970s.

The artwork is printed directly onto the mirrored glass surface and is surrounded by a deep burgundy mirrored border, fine gold-coloured detailing and a profiled gold-coloured frame.

The inscription CHAMPAGNE LOUIS ROEDERER appears beneath the illustration.

The reverse features a cardboard backing secured to the frame with metal retaining clips.

Suitable for champagne advertising collections, restaurants, wine bars, home bars or decorative display.
