Veuve Clicquot “Clicquot in the Snow” – The Winter Campaign, the Yellow Ski Jacket and Branded Ski Gear

Veuve Clicquot “Clicquot in the Snow” – The History of the Winter Campaign, the Yellow Ski Jacket and Branded Ski Equipment

Skiing, snow-covered mountain peaks, sunlit terraces and a glass of champagne – in 2007, Veuve Clicquot launched its “Clicquot in the Snow” campaign, bringing champagne into the world of winter sports and après-ski culture. What began as a promotional initiative developed into an international series of events, accompanied over the years by yellow ski jackets, ski boots, skis, snowboards and a variety of winter accessories.

2007 – The Birth of “Clicquot in the Snow” in Aspen

The story began in 2007 in Aspen, one of America’s best-known ski resorts. Veuve Clicquot wanted to introduce champagne into a new setting, extending its association beyond traditional festive dinners and formal receptions to the relaxed social atmosphere of après-ski.

The idea was simple: after a day on the slopes, guests could enjoy champagne on mountain restaurant terraces, at outdoor champagne bars and during winter events.

The snowy landscape and Veuve Clicquot’s signature yellow complemented each other. The brand’s presence soon extended beyond champagne bottles and serving accessories. Yellow parasols, branded blankets, clothing and sports equipment began appearing alongside the ski slopes.

Following Aspen, the event series expanded to other well-known ski destinations, including Kitzbühel, Thredbo in Australia, Queenstown in New Zealand and Niseko in Japan.

Winter champagne experiences gradually became a distinct part of Veuve Clicquot’s marketing, accompanied by collaborations with fashion labels and winter sports equipment manufacturers.

The First Winter Collections

2010 – Veuve Clicquot × Smith Optics Ski Goggles

Among the campaign’s earliest well-documented sports accessories were ski goggles developed in collaboration between Smith Optics and Veuve Clicquot.

Introduced in 2010, the model combined Smith Optics’ sports technology with Veuve Clicquot’s branding. The goggles featured a Carbonic-X lens and ventilation system, making them suitable for actual skiing rather than merely serving as promotional merchandise.

2011 – DKNY × Veuve Clicquot Yellow Boots

Another collaboration followed in 2011, this time with the fashion brand DKNY.

The two companies introduced limited-edition yellow rubber boots, reflecting Veuve Clicquot’s signature brand color.

The boots reflected the direction of the campaign: Veuve Clicquot wanted its brand identity to be present not only in champagne service but also in what guests wore.

2011 – Snowboards, Snow Tubes and Winter Sports Equipment

Between January 1 and March 31, 2011, Veuve Clicquot organized a “Clicquot in the Snow” sweepstakes in the United States.

A total of 90 grand prizes were announced, consisting of three types of winter sports equipment:

The prize draw took place on April 4, 2011.

The inflatable snow tubes offered another way to enjoy snowy slopes, allowing participants to slide downhill on circular, air-filled equipment.

With their yellow branding, the snowboards and other winter sports accessories became part of the campaign’s visual identity.

2012 – Marc Jacobs × Veuve Clicquot Cashmere Gloves

In November 2012, the Marc Jacobs fashion house joined Veuve Clicquot’s winter collaborations.

The result was a pair of yellow, fingerless cashmere gloves, combining fashionable cold-weather accessories with the practicalities of enjoying champagne outdoors.

Their fingerless design allowed the wearer to hold a champagne glass comfortably while keeping the palms and backs of the hands warm.

The collaboration was covered by both Vogue and Glamour, making it one of the campaign’s well-documented fashion accessories.

The Expansion of Veuve Clicquot Winter Clothing and Accessories

Over time, Veuve Clicquot’s winter clothing and accessories became increasingly varied.

Knitted hats, gloves and earmuffs were produced in the brand’s yellow-and-black color scheme. Many featured the VCP monogram and the anchor emblem associated with the champagne house.

The winter hat collection included simple knitted designs, turned-up brim styles and pieces developed in collaboration with other fashion brands. The Veuve Clicquot × Fusalp collaboration, for example, included cream-colored wool hats with yellow stripes.

These accessories were not all released in the same year or as part of a single collection. Some were associated with winter events, while others came from collaborations with fashion brands or promotional campaigns.

2018 – Kingswood × Veuve Clicquot Skis

One of the campaign’s notable collaborations with a winter sports equipment manufacturer was introduced in New Zealand in 2018.

Veuve Clicquot partnered with Kingswood Skis to produce a limited series of skis connected to the “Clicquot in the Snow” events in Queenstown.

The skis featured Kingswood’s bamboo-core construction, while their surfaces displayed graphics inspired by Veuve Clicquot’s Colourama designs.

The collection comprised ten numbered graphic editions, available from June 2018.

These skis were intended both as functional sports equipment and as expressions of the champagne house’s visual identity.

2019 – The Appearance of the Yellow Veuve Clicquot Ski Jacket

In 2019, Veuve Clicquot’s yellow quilted ski jackets appeared at “Clicquot in the Snow” events, becoming recognizable items within the brand’s winter clothing range.

The jackets featured high stand-up collars and thick padding, designed for cold and snowy conditions. Their signature yellow exterior was complemented by black zippers, snap buttons and gold-colored embroidery.

The Veuve Clicquot name appeared on the chest, while one upper sleeve displayed the VCP emblem incorporating the brand’s anchor symbol.

Practical details included a two-way front zipper, a snap-button storm flap, zippered pockets and adjustable cuffs.

The jackets fitted naturally into the visual setting of the winter events. Their yellow color made them easily recognizable against the snow-covered slopes, mountain restaurant terraces and outdoor champagne bars.

A 2019 report by Ski Canada specifically mentioned Veuve Clicquot ski jackets and branded skis.

The jackets were produced in several sizes, and surviving examples can now be found on the collectors’ market for Veuve Clicquot branded clothing.

2019 – “Clicquot in the Snow” in Japan and New Zealand

By 2019, the campaign had grown into an international series of events spanning several continents.

From March 1 to 10, the Japanese ski resort of Niseko hosted its local “Veuve Clicquot In the Snow” celebrations. The program involved seven hospitality venues, featuring mountain champagne bars, restaurant collaborations and outdoor events.

That same August, Queenstown in New Zealand hosted the Clicquot winter festival for the ninth time.

One of the events was the Veuve Clicquot dog-sledding experience, held in the Queenstown region from August 1 to 4, 2019, as part of the “Clicquot in the Snow” festival.

The dog-sledding activities took place at Snow Farm in the Cardrona Valley, where participants could experience husky-powered sled rides against the backdrop of New Zealand’s snow-covered mountains.

The wider program also featured champagne tastings, mountain picnics, fashion shows and winter sports activities.

Yellow clothing, ski equipment and branded accessories formed part of the visual identity of these outdoor events.

2025 – Veuve Clicquot × Elan Skis

In 2024, Veuve Clicquot announced another ski collaboration, this time with the Slovenian manufacturer Elan.

The collection, planned for release in 2025, combined the champagne house’s yellow color scheme with Elan’s experience and technology in ski manufacturing.

The collaboration continued the idea behind the earlier Kingswood ski project: rather than simply placing a logo on sports equipment, Veuve Clicquot’s visual identity became part of the overall product design.

Overview of Veuve Clicquot Winter Releases

Over the years, the following winter products have been documented or associated with Veuve Clicquot promotional campaigns:

  • Ski jackets: Yellow quilted winter jackets featuring the VCP emblem, appearing at events in 2019.
  • Ski collections: Kingswood in 2018 and the Elan collaboration announced for 2025.
  • Snowboards: Promotional editions featured in the 2011 American campaign.
  • Snow tubes: Branded inflatable snow tubes offered as promotional prizes in 2011.
  • Ski goggles: Smith Optics collaboration, 2010.
  • Winter boots: DKNY yellow rubber boots, 2011.
  • Gloves: Marc Jacobs cashmere gloves, 2012, alongside other branded knitted models.
  • Winter hats: Knitted designs featuring the VCP emblem, including pieces associated with the Fusalp collaboration.
  • Earmuffs: Branded accessories connected to winter promotional campaigns.
  • Picnic blankets and throws: Used during outdoor champagne events and mountain picnics.

Some of these products were commercially available, while others were distributed to event guests, offered as competition prizes or produced specifically for promotional purposes.

The Legacy of “Clicquot in the Snow”

Launched in 2007, Veuve Clicquot’s winter campaign gradually developed into something far beyond conventional champagne advertising.

Champagne bars beside the ski slopes, mountain picnics, fashion shows and branded sports equipment created a recognizable winter identity for the champagne house.

Yellow ski jackets, skis and gloves played an important role in this development. They were worn and used by participants while also making the Veuve Clicquot brand visible throughout the ski resorts.

Today, surviving examples of these winter collections attract interest among collectors, particularly pieces associated with documented campaigns or collaborations.

The history of “Clicquot in the Snow” illustrates how après-ski culture became part of Veuve Clicquot’s international brand identity.

The connection between the signature yellow color, winter sports and champagne continued through later events and product releases, establishing a visual tradition that extended well beyond the original Aspen campaign.
