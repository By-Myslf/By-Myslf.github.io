About this piece

Moët & Chandon “Nautical Star” Convertible Champagne Carrier & Ice Bucket – 2007 – Unused – Original Box

Its distinctive five-pointed star design is not merely decorative. By unzipping the red zippers, the sides fold open within seconds to create a full-size star-shaped ice bucket, designed to hold and chill one 0.75 L champagne bottle on ice.

The exterior is made from strong off-white canvas with black reinforced details and contrasting bright red zippers. A substantial twisted-rope handle is fitted with a black grip bearing the Moët & Chandon Champagne name. Moët & Chandon branding is also embossed into the black star-shaped base.

The carrier has never been used.

What makes this example particularly difficult to find is the survival of its original factory protection. The original protective film and tissue-paper coverings are still present around the zippers, indicating that the piece was stored rather than used.

The interior is clean and shows no evidence of use.

Even more unusually, the original large Moët & Chandon presentation/storage box is included. These boxes were often discarded, so finding the Star Bag unused and still accompanied by its original packaging is uncommon.

The original cardboard box is finished in an off-white and black colour scheme with large Moët & Chandon branding.

The printed graphics illustrate the Star Bag both as a bottle carrier and opened into its characteristic star-shaped champagne cooler. The packaging also retains the original French and English promotional text describing its use aboard a yacht, together with the original product/barcode label.

The box shows age-related storage wear, including some surface marks, rubbing and discolouration visible in the photographs. It nevertheless remains complete and is an important part of the original presentation.

The Star Bag itself is in unused condition with original protective materials still in place.

Bottle not included.

A scarce surviving example of a 2007 Moët & Chandon promotional accessory, particularly difficult to find unused, with its original protective materials and original illustrated cardboard box still together.
