Labels, menus, price lists and advertising are often the most affordable champagne collectables, and they can tell you a great deal about how champagne was made and sold.

## Sweetness words

Champagne in the 19th century was usually much sweeter than today, and the labels show it. Words such as *doux*, *demi-sec* and *sec* all describe sweet or fairly sweet wines. *Brut*, meaning very dry, appears from the later 19th century and gradually became the most common style.

## Printing

Many older labels were printed by lithography, often in several colours with gold. Look closely with a magnifier: lithographic printing shows soft, grainy areas of colour, while later offset printing shows a regular pattern of tiny dots.

## Paper and condition

Old paper is often slightly uneven and may show light brown spots, called foxing. Labels removed from bottles usually have glue residue or damage on the back. Unused labels, from printers' or producers' stocks, are cleaner and especially welcome.

## Storing labels

Keep them flat in acid-free sleeves or albums, out of direct light. Don't try to remove old glue with water, and never use sticky tape for repairs.
