Starting a collection of muselet caps

The little metal disc on top of a champagne cork is one of the most affordable ways into collecting. What to look for, how to sort them and how to keep them in good condition.

By Guest Author, Muselet collector. Published 28 September 2026

Sample article: replace this with the real text.

Every bottle of champagne is closed with a wire cage, the muselet, which holds the cork against the pressure inside. On top of the cage sits a small metal disc, the plaque de muselet. Producers print them with their name, initials, a coat of arms, a vintage or a special design, and many issue new ones every year.

Collecting them even has its own name: placomusophilia.

Where to begin

Most collectors start with what they drink, then trade with friends and visit specialist fairs, which are held regularly in the Champagne region. A focused theme makes a collection more satisfying than a random heap: one producer, one village, one decade, or a single subject such as animals or anniversaries.

What makes a cap desirable

  • Age: older caps, especially from before the Second World War, are scarcer.
  • Small producers: growers who made only a few thousand bottles left far fewer caps than the large houses.
  • Condition: look for bright printing without scratches, and a flat, undented surface.
  • Errors and short runs: misprints and limited editions are sought after.

Removing and storing them

Bend the cage gently to free the cap rather than prising it off, which leaves a crease. Store caps flat, in trays or albums made for coins or caps, away from damp. Never clean the printed surface with anything stronger than a soft, dry cloth.
