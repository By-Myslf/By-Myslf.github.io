Caring for silver-plated ice buckets

Ice, meltwater and over-enthusiastic polishing are the enemies of an old champagne bucket. A conservator's advice on cleaning, using and displaying them.

By Guest Author, Metalwork conservator. Published 10 September 2026

Sample article: replace this with the real text.

Most antique champagne buckets are not solid silver but silver plate: a thin layer of silver over copper or a copper alloy. That layer is easily worn away, and once the base metal shows through, it cannot be put back without replating.

Using a bucket

Old buckets were made to be used, and there is no reason not to. Empty the meltwater as soon as the evening is over, rinse the inside with clean water and dry it straight away. Water left standing overnight, especially with salt added to chill the bottle faster, causes dark stains and pitting.

Cleaning

  • Wash in warm water with a few drops of mild washing-up liquid, then dry with a soft cloth.
  • Never use a dishwasher. The detergents dull silver and attack the joints.
  • Polish only when necessary, with a gentle silver polish and light pressure. Every polish removes a little silver, and worn high points are where the copper shows first.

Display

A bucket on open display tarnishes slowly in dry air. A closed cabinet with an anti-tarnish strip keeps it bright for much longer. Avoid storing it in newspaper, felt or rubber bands, which contain sulphur and speed up tarnishing.

Should you replate?

Replating makes a bucket look new, but it also removes the history of the surface and can reduce its value to collectors. For a good antique piece, ask a specialist before deciding.
